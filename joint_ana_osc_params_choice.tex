\section {Choice of oscillation parameters}
\label{sec:osc_params_choice}


This analysis departs from choices which have been followed historically in the T2K stand-alone $\nu_{e}$-appearance and 
$\nu_{\mu}$-disappearance analyses by changing the oscillation mixing parameters and the mass-squared splitting parameters.
There is a strong motivation for this change in the context of a joint 3-flavour analysis, and it is outlined below.

\subsection* {Neutrino mixing parameters}

In the stand-alone $\nu_{\mu}$-disappearance analysis, $\sin^{2}2\theta_{23}$ was the variable typically measured since it appears in the first-order approximate formula for the $\nu_{\mu}$ survival probability:
\begin{equation}
P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu}) \approx 1 - sin^{2}2\theta_{23} sin^{2} \left(  \frac{\Delta m_{32}^{2} L}{4E_{\nu}}\right)
\end{equation}
In the latest stand-alone $\nu_{\mu}$-disappearance analysis \cite{OurRun1234DisappAnalysis3Flavour}, however, if was found that the choice of octant (first octant with $\theta_{23} < \pi/4$ or second octant with $\theta_{23}>pi/4$) had a significant effect in the confidence regions. Therefore, the latest T2K stand-alone $\nu_{\mu}$-disappearance analysis already switched to fit $\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ instead of $\sin^{2}2\theta_{23}$.

Looking at the leading-order approximation of $P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e})$, the 23-sector mixing parameter appears as $\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$:
\begin{equation}
P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e}) \approx sin^{2}2\theta_{13}sin^{2}\theta_{23} sin^{2} \left(  \frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2} L}{4E_{\nu}}\right)
\label{eq:pnumunue}
\end{equation}

So in the context of the joint fit it makes even more sense to use $\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ since it is possible to lift the octant degeneracy by combining the $\nu_{\mu}$-disappearance and $\nu_{e}$-appearance analyses, as shown 
graphically in Fig.\ \ref{fig:ambig}. 

Thus, the natural choice for the joint 3-flavour oscillation analysis to fit for 
$\sin^{2}\theta_{ij}$ rather than $\sin^{2}2\theta_{ij}$, and this is the choice that has been used in the VALOR joint analysis since the previous analysis described in \cite{OurJointAnalysis}.

Recently the question of fitting $\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}$ instead of $\sin^{2}\theta_{13}$ was pointed out within the oscillation analysis group. 
On the one hand, $\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}$ appears in the leading term or the $P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e})$ oscillation probability formula \ref{eq:pnumunue}.
But there $\cos^{2}\theta_{13}$ appears in the approximate formula for $P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu})$ oscillation probability:

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu}) &\approx 1 - 4 \sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2} L}{4E}\right) c_{13}^{2}s_{23}^{2} \left[ s_{12}^{2}c_{23}^{2}+c_{12}^{2}s_{13}^{2}s_{23}^{2}+c_{12}^{2}c_{23}^{2}+s_{12}^{2}s_{13}^{2}s_{23}^{2}\right] \\
&= 1 - 4 \sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2} L}{4E}\right) c_{13}^{2}s_{23}^{2} \left(s_{12}^{2} + c_{12}^{2} \right) \left[ c_{23}^{2} + s_{13}^{2}s_{23}^{2} \right] \\
&= 1 - 4 \sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2} L}{4E}\right) c_{13}^{2}s_{23}^{2} \left[ c_{23}^{2} + s_{13}^{2}s_{23}^{2}\right]\\
&= 1 - \sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2} L}{4E}\right) \left[ \cos^{2}\theta_{13}\sin^{2}2\theta_{23} +\sin^{4}\theta_{23}\sin^{2}2\theta_{13} \right]
\end{split}
\label{numusurv3fapprox}
\end{equation}

Therefore, although $P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e})$ is approximately linear with $\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}$, $P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu})$ is not, so it is not clear that switching to $\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}$ would be the natural choice for the joint fit.
Furthermore, after several checks, it was found that there was no visible difference in the results obtained fitting $\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}$ directly or fitting $\sin^{2}\theta_{13}$ and making the appropriate re-scaling that could indicate an easier fit when using $\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}$. Examples are given in Sec. \ref{sec:fake_datasets} and \ref{sec:results}.

On the other hand, in the context of global fits like the ones in Ref. \cite{LisiGlobalAnalysis} or \cite{ConchaMaltoniGlobalAnalysis}, the variable used is $\sin^{2}\theta_{13}$, as it was in this variable that MINOS reported its joint fit's results in NNN 2013 \cite{MINOSjoint}.

For these reasons, results of the VALOR joint oscillation analysis will be given mainly presented in $\sin^{2}\theta_{13}$.
In any case, the VALOR framework has been updated to fit the three options:
\begin{itemize}
\item{($\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$,$\sin^{2}\theta_{13}$)}
\item{($\sin^{2}2\theta_{23}$,$\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}$)}
\item{($\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$,$\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}$)}
\end{itemize}

\subsection* {Mass-squared splitting parameters}
\label{sec:masschoice}

Previous T2K analyses have used the following mass-squared splitting parameters:
\begin{center}
$\left( |\Delta m_{32}^{2}|, \Delta m_{21}^{2} \right)$ 
\end{center}
which are defined as $\Delta m_{ij}^{2} = m_{i}^{2} - m_{j}^{2}$ according to the PDG notation \cite{PDG}. 
The mass hierarchy is set through the sign of $\Delta m_{32}^{2}$
i.e.\ $\Delta m_{32}^{2} = |\Delta m_{32}^{2}|$ in the normal hierarchy 
and $\Delta m_{32}^{2} = - |\Delta m_{32}^{2}|$ in the inverted hierarchy. 
The remaining mass-squared splitting is defined as $\Delta m_{31}^{2} = \Delta m_{32}^{2} + \Delta m_{21}^{2}$ for both hierarchies.

In the normal hierarchy (NH) $m_{1}^{2}<m_{2}^{2}<<m_{3}^{2}$, while in the inverted hierarchy (IH) $m_{3}^{2}<<m_{1}^{2}<m_{2}^{2}$,
i.e.\ $|\Delta m_{31}^{2}|$ is the largest mass-squared splitting in the NH, whereas $|\Delta m_{32}^{2}|$ is the largest in the IH. 
This means that it is not possible to compare the two hierarchies if the atmospheric mass-squared difference is input as 
$|\Delta m_{32}^{2}|$. This has been pointed out by several authors and different schemes are typically employed in global
oscillation fits to circumvent this issue. In the latest stand-alone $\nu_{\mu}$-disappearance analysis \cite{OurRun1234DisappAnalysis3Flavour}, for instance, results comparing both mass hierarchies were presented in such a way that the Y axis showing $|\Delta m^{2}|$ had a different meaning for normal hierarchy, $\Delta m^{2}_{32}$, and inverted hierarchy, $\Delta m^{2}_{13}$.

The VALOR joint oscillation analysis uses the scheme defined by Fogli and Lisi in 
\cite{LisiGlobalAnalysis} \cite{Fogli} in which $\left( |\Delta m_{32}^{2}|, \Delta m_{21}^{2} \right)$ are replaced by:
\begin{center}
$\left( |\Delta m^{2}|, \delta m^{2} \right)$
\end{center}
where 
$\Delta m^{2} = \left(m_{3}^{2} - \frac {m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}} {2} \right) =  \left( \frac {\Delta m_{31}^{2} + \Delta m_{32}^{2}} {2} \right)$
and 
$\delta m^{2} = \Delta m_{21}^{2}$.
$|\Delta m^{2}|$ is the "atmospheric" squared-mass splitting, which has the same absolute value for both mass hierarchies.
$\delta m^{2}$ is the "solar" squared-mass splitting which is known to be positive. When the mass-squared splittings are input as 
$\left(\Delta m^{2}, \delta m^{2} \right)$, the mass hierarchy is set through the sign of $|\Delta m^{2}|$, and $\Delta m_{32}^{2}$ 
and $\Delta m_{31}^{2}$ are calculated as 
\begin{displaymath}
\begin{split}
\Delta m_{32}^{2} &= \Delta m^{2} - \frac{\delta m^{2}}{2}  \\
\Delta m_{31}^{2} &= \Delta m^{2} + \frac{\delta m^{2}}{2}
\end{split}
\end{displaymath}
for both mass hierarchies. The ability to input the mass-squared differences as $\left( |\Delta m_{32}^{2}|, \Delta m_{21}^{2} \right)$ has also been retained for 
backward compatibility and to allow various validation checks.

The effects of changing the convention for the inputs of the mass-squared splitting parameters on P($\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu}$) 
and P($\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e}$) are shown in Fig.\ \ref{fig:oscprobmassdef}.

\subsection* {Treatment of solar (12-sector) oscillation parameters}

A study has been made in which $\sin^{2}2\theta_{12}$ and $\Delta m_{21}^{2}$ were allowed to float in the fit of the single $\mu$-like ring Super K sample, and 
the results are shown in Appendix \ref{app:solar} and Appendix F of \cite{OurJointAnalysis}. It was found that when these parameters were allowed to float freely, the fitter returned extreme values of these parameter because there is no sensitivity in the single $\mu$-like ring sample nor the single e-like ring sample to constrain them, being those extreme values far away from the values established by different experiments. When they were allowed to float including the constraint known from well established measurements instead, there was no effect in the results obtained (details of these tests are given in Appendix \ref{app:solar}). 
For this reason, these parameters were fixed throughout this analysis to the values 
in table \ref{tab:solarparameters}.
