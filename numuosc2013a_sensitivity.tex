\section {Results of 6.3933$\times$10$^{20}$ POT fitter validation}% and sensitivity studies }
\label{sec:sensitivity}

The performance of the fitter was evaluated using an ensemble of 5.1k toy MC experiments,
generated
with an exposure of 6.3933$\times$10$^{20}$ POT,
and input oscillation parameters
$|\Delta m^{2}_{32}|$ = 2.4$\times$10$^{-3}$ eV$^{2}$/c$^{4}$
and $\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ = 0.37. A further 5.1k toy MC experiments were
created for each of the two cases $\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ = 0.513 and $\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ = 0.65.
A further set of toy MC experiments with the same input oscillation parameters and an exposure of
7.8$\times$10$^{21}$ POT (the T2K ultimate POT goal) were also used.
These simulated experiments included statistical fluctuations and
fluctuations of all 44 systematic parameters
and $\delta_{CP}$.
The toy MC experiments were fitted using MINUIT and the MINUIT HESSE errors were computed.
Residual and pull\footnote{pull = $(f_{bf} - f_{true}) / \sigma_{fit}$}
distributions were studied for oscillation and systematic parameters.

Residual distributions for $\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ and $|\Delta m^{2}_{32}|$ are shown in
Figs.\ \ref{fig:syst_residuals:oscparam:run1234a:0.37}, \ref{fig:syst_residuals:oscparam:run1234a:0.513}, and \ref{fig:syst_residuals:oscparam:run1234a:0.65}
for 6.3933$\times$10$^{20}$ POT.
A clear three peak structure is seen in $\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$, with peaks occurring at
the input point, the mirror point, and the point where maximum disappearance occurs\footnote{
  In the 3-flavour oscillation formula\footnotemark
  $\:P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu})
  \approx 1 - \left( \cos^4(\theta_{13})\sin^{2}(2\theta_{23}) + \sin^{2}(\theta_{23})\sin^{2}(2\theta_{13}) \right) \sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2} L}{4E}\right)$,
  the maximum amount of disappearance occurs not at $\sin^{2}(\theta_{23}) = 0.5$, but it is offset slightly due to the next-to-leading order term.
  The extremum occurs at $\sin^{2}(\theta_{23})= \frac{1}{2(1-\sin^{2}(\theta_{13}))} \approx 0.513$.
  In addition, the oscillation probability is approximately symmetric about this point, meaning that for every input value $!= 0.513$, there exists
  a mirror point with a similar oscillation probability.
  More discussion of this effect can be found in Appendix E of \cite{OurRun123JointOscillationAnalysis}.
}\footnotetext{In the fits, we use full 3-flavour oscillation formulae, and not this approximation.
    See and Appendix A in \cite{OurRun123DisappAnalysis3Flavour} for full details.}.
Figs.\ \ref{fig:syst_residuals:oscparam:potgoal:0.37}, \ref{fig:syst_residuals:oscparam:potgoal:0.513}, and \ref{fig:syst_residuals:oscparam:potgoal:0.65}
are the corresponding plots for 7.8$\times$10$^{21}$ POT.
The three-peak structure is lost with high statistics;
there are peaks only at the input point and the mirror point.

Residual distributions for the systematic parameters are shown in
Figs.\ \ref{fig:syst_residuals1:run1234a:0.37}-\ref{fig:syst_residuals4:run1234a:0.37} for
$\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ = 0.37 and $|\Delta m^{2}_{32}|$ = 2.4$\times$10$^{-3}$ eV$^{2}$/c$^{4}$,
Figs.\ \ref{fig:syst_residuals1:run1234a:0.513}-\ref{fig:syst_residuals4:run1234a:0.513} for
$\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ = 0.513 and $|\Delta m^{2}_{32}|$ = 2.4$\times$10$^{-3}$ eV$^{2}$/c$^{4}$,
and
Figs.\ \ref{fig:syst_residuals1:run1234a:0.65}-\ref{fig:syst_residuals4:run1234a:0.65} for
$\sin^{2}\theta_{23}$ = 0.65 and $|\Delta m^{2}_{32}|$ = 2.4$\times$10$^{-3}$ eV$^{2}$/c$^{4}$.
The corresponding pull plots are shown in
Figs.\ \ref{fig:syst_pulls1:run1234a:0.37}-\ref{fig:syst_pulls4:run1234a:0.37},
Figs.\ \ref{fig:syst_pulls1:run1234a:0.513}-\ref{fig:syst_pulls4:run1234a:0.513}, and
Figs.\ \ref{fig:syst_pulls1:run1234a:0.65}-\ref{fig:syst_pulls4:run1234a:0.65}.

No problem was identified with the fitter performance, which performed as expected.
There are biases in the fitted - true values for 3 systematic parameters, namely spectral function, $\pi$-less 
$\Delta$ decay and the NC bin in the SuperK detector efficiency + FSI + SI matrix,
but these biases are understood. The bias in the spectral function 
systematic is due to its true input values being drawn from a uniform distribution between 0 and 1 rather than 
from a Gaussian distribution. The bias in $\pi$-less $\Delta$ decay is due to the method of calculation of event 
weights when this parameter is varied. By default, 20\% of $\Delta$s decay in this way in NEUT, and the uncertainty 
is 20\%. This means that it is unphysical to tweak this parameter to be more negative than -1$\sigma$, and 
consequently there is an asymmetry in weights between positive tweaks (which change continuously as the tweak 
increases) and negative tweaks (for which all tweaks more negative than -1$\sigma$ are assigned the same weight 
as that for a -1$\sigma$ tweak). The bias in the NC bin in the SuperK detector efficiency + FSI + SI bin matrix
comes from the low statistics at current POT.
When Poisson fluctuations are made of the bin contents of the $E_{reco}$ distributions, a downward fluctuation is more likely than an upward
fluctuation (see replies to comments and revision history for TN141 \cite{OurRun123DisappAnalysis3Flavour}).
Adding the fact that the NC modes are distributed around the oscillation maximum, the (most likely) downwards Poisson fluctuations translate into a (most likely)
negative value of the NC efficiency, explaining a bias to negative values for this parameter.
As can be seen from the plots, when moving away from the point of maximal disappearance as input
(and so reducing the depth of the oscillation dip),
the NC bin in the SuperK detector efficiency + FSI + SI matrix bias is reduced.
